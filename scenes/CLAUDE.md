# scenes

Game scenes.

- `Run.tscn` (Node2D, main scene, `Run.gd`) holds, in this draw order, `StarfieldView`, `AtmosphereView`, `GravityHaloView`, `WellView`, `RingView`, `AsteroidView`, `SmallObjectView`, 4 `Body.tscn` instances (`Planet` index 0, `Moon1`..`Moon3` index 1..3; a moon node hides when the generated picture has fewer moons; discs draw over stars, glow, halo, strokes, ring, asteroids and small bodies and under the carrier, the beam, the ship and overlays), an instance of `Carrier.tscn` (`Carrier`, under the beam and the ship), `BeamView`, `CollectorView`, `DistortionView`, an instance of `Ship.tscn`, the `Prediction` `Line2D`, `ReferenceMarker`, `FlightVector`, the run-level `FlightCamera`, `SoundView` (a plain `Node`, right before the `CanvasLayer`) and a `CanvasLayer` with a `ui/TuningPanel.tscn` instance (hidden on start, screen-fixed) a `ui/HUD.tscn` instance (well scale, ignores mouse and focus) and, after it, a `ui/Summary.tscn` instance (end-of-flight summary, hidden on start).
- `Run.gd` (Node2D root of `Run.tscn`): `_ready` calls `Tuning.regenerate(Telemetry.seed, Telemetry.flight)`, so a fresh start runs a generated planet-plus-moons picture (seed 1, flight 1) instead of the planet-only code default. It owns nothing else.
- `Ship.tscn` is a `CharacterBody2D` with `Ship.gd`, a `Visual` `Node2D` (script `ShipView.gd`; the 3D model `assets/models/ship.glb` in its own `Layer3D` plus the `Flame` and `Glow` polygons, nose toward local -Y) and a `CollisionShape2D` with a `CircleShape2D` (unchanged). It has no camera: there is one camera, `FlightCamera`.
- `Palette.gd` (`class_name Palette`, `RefCounted`) is the one colour table (meaning-bound, style frame W7): `BACKGROUND` #0B0F1A (equals the `default_clear_color` project setting, pinned by a test), `ICE` #7FC8F8 (ring, top asteroid tier), `ORE` #F2A93B (cargo, asteroids), `ORE_BRIGHT` #FFE27A (unique ore), `ORE_BASE` #8A7D6B (base ore), `FUEL` #8FE0A0 (fuel cells in the HUD tank), `STAR` #9BA8C4 (starfield), `ORE_RARE` #F8C65A (rare ore), `DANGER` #FF5A36 (atmosphere glow and edge, ceiling, flame, nozzle heat), `HALO` #B8D8C0 (gravity halo, orbits, prediction, speed chevron, reference marker, UI lines), `SAFE` #FFD45C (carrier orbit and marker, tow boundary, HUD ship marker, ship crosshair at zoom), `OBOD_ACCENT` #C9A27A («Обод» amber: the planet disc and the ring boulders), `MOON` neutral grey, `HULL` (ship hull) and `HULL_SHADE` (wings), `OUTLINE` (= `HALO`, the style outline and facet edges). Static `shade(c)` multiplies rgb by `SHADE_FACTOR` 0.35 (alpha kept); `LIGHT_DIR` is the unit Vector2 to the star in the carrier's rotating frame (fixed there, so on screen the terminator turns with the camera; tech-debt #35); `LIGHT_ELEVATION` 0.35 is its z toward the viewer for the disc and 3D shaders; `lit_half(radius, n, light_dir) -> PackedVector2Array` gives n+1 points of the lit half-circle (terminator through the centre perpendicular to `light_dir`). `LAVA` (1.0, 0.35, 0.1; planet night cracks) and `HAZE` (1.0, 0.86, 0.7; planet limb haze). Views read their hue from `Palette` and keep their own alpha and stroke constants; shader uniform defaults match it.
- Flight physics is the pure `ShipFlight.step(state, input, t, dt, snapshot = null, report = null) -> ShipState` (`class_name`, static, no scene dependency, so trajectory prediction can reuse it). It returns a new state, never mutates its arguments, and is deterministic. A Dictionary passed as `report` gets `"transferred"` (whether this tick's transfer moved ice; `Ship` counts `Telemetry.transfers` from it), `"damage"` (`{atmosphere, moon, asteroid, piece}`, hull lost this tick by cause, the hull delta around each contact call) and `"heat_limited"` (thrust was cut by heat this tick); `Telemetry.sample` reads them. Prediction passes no report. `ShipParams` are read from `Tuning.ship` on each call, no cache. Any new flight feature goes into the state and `step`, not into the `Ship` node.
- The carrier (`матка`) is not a body and not a gravity source: it patrols its orbit IN the rotating frame as a pure formula of t. Public `ShipFlight` statics (read `Tuning` per call like their siblings): `carrier_angle(t)` = `ω_c·t` with `ω_c = Tuning.planet.carrier_patrol_speed / carrier_orbit_radius` (0 with the knob at 0 or a degenerate radius), `carrier_pos(t)` = `carrier_orbit_radius · (cos, sin)(carrier_angle(t))`, `carrier_vel(t)` = `carrier_patrol_speed · (−sin, cos)(carrier_angle(t))` — counter-clockwise, exactly the knob magnitude. Being pure statics of t used by both live flight and prediction, prediction == flight holds automatically. Views read the same statics (`WellView` marker, `ReferenceMarker` ring, `CarrierView`), so the drawn carrier is always the flight carrier.
- Step order: rotation, control branch, heat, atmospheric drag, ring drag, translation, beam (`_beam`: charge and break, only acts with `input.beam`), moon contact, asteroid contact, small objects (`SmallObjects.update`, then `_collect` pull, then `_small_contact`: collectible pieces into the hold, other bodies swallow / push), hull and crash, then (only when not crashed: a crash wins) the docking check. A `docked` state skips all of it (see Docking). The control branch is an elif chain, soft brake > drift sync > boost / thrust; held actions do not add up. Rotation is manual `turn` or, with `has_heading`, a critically damped PD toward `target_heading`; spin damping is always on; `rot` stays wrapped to [-PI, PI]. Thrust acts along the nose `Vector2.UP.rotated(rot)`. Translation is semi-implicit Euler in the rotating frame: gravity, `frame_accel`, thrust and brake terms and, when `dock_armed`, the carrier pull, then position. A ship at rest at the stationary carrier (patrol 0) stays there: `g_eff(r_c) = 0` still holds, the patrol moves the carrier within the frame, it does not bend the field. Accepted cost: explicit Coriolis in semi-implicit Euler drifts band-drift orbits at large Ω_f; D35-scale Ω_f is not affected.
- `HoldCell.gd` (`class_name`, `RefCounted`): one hold cell, `type: StringName` (key of `Tuning.resources`), `fill` 0..1, `copy()`. `ShipState.hold` and `ShipState.tank` are `Array[HoldCell]`, deep-copied by `ShipState.copy()` (prediction == flight). `ShipState._init(with_loadout := true)` puts `Tuning.ship.loadout_fuel_cells` full `fuel` cells into the tank through `Hold.add`, clamped to `tank_capacity`; `copy()` builds with `false`. `Ship.respawn()` gets the loadout through `ShipState.new()`.
- `Hold.gd` (`class_name`, static): `mass(s)` = `hull_mass` + Σ `fill` × `Tuning.resources[type].mass` over hold and tank; `used_volume(cells)` counts a partial cell's full volume; `add(s, type, amount) -> float` tops up the last partial cell of that type, then opens new cells while Σ volume ≤ capacity (`fuel` goes to `s.tank` with `tank_capacity`, every other type to `s.hold` with `hold_capacity`), returns the amount actually added. All read `Tuning` per call. `energy(s) -> float` is the burnable energy: Σ `fill` × `Tuning.resources[type].energy` over all `s.tank` cells (fuel and transferred ice; D38). `drain(s, amount) -> float` burns up to `amount` of tank energy and returns what it drained: ice cells first (partial, then full), then fuel cells (partial, then full), each with its own per-cell energy (no partial cell is left behind); the order is the public `burn_order(cells)`, `HUD.tank_slots` draws its reverse; a cell whose fill drops to ≤ `EPS` (1e-6) is erased, freeing its slot and mass. `room(s, type) -> float` is the amount `add` would take of `type` right now (tank for fuel) without changing the state; the collector uses it. Jettison picks (M7, D42): `heaviest(s)` is the `s.hold` index of max `fill` × type mass, ties to the lowest index, -1 for an empty hold; `effective_pick(s)` is `s.jettison_pick`, or `heaviest` when it is -1 or out of range; `next_pick(s)` is `effective_pick + 1` wrapping over `s.hold` (unchanged pick for an empty hold); `remove_at(s, i)` removes and returns a hold cell. Hold cells never burn. Transfer (M9, D38): `can_transfer(s) -> bool` and `transfer(s) -> bool` move one hold ice cell (the partial one, else the first; `jettison_pick` is not used to choose, but the removal shifts it so it keeps pointing at the same cell: removed index < pick → pick − 1, removed index == pick → -1, back to heaviest). With `Tuning.ship.refiner == false` the cell goes into `s.tank` as a separate ice cell of the same fill (needs a free tank slot; energy 1 per cell); with `refiner == true` it becomes `fill · E_ice / E_fuel` fuel via `add` (1 ice → 1/3 fuel). All or nothing: without room both return false and nothing changes.
- `Fuel.gd` (`class_name`, static): `per_dv() -> float` = `climb_coefficient / sqrt(2 · 0.45 · ΔΦ)`, with ΔΦ = `planet_potential_eff(carrier_orbit_radius) − planet_potential_eff(WellGenerator.tiers().boundaries[0])` (the atmosphere edge). The 0.45 is the middle of the middle tier (45 % of the swing below the carrier), so a clean climb from there costs ≈ `climb_coefficient` × mass. Reads `Tuning` per call, so it follows the gravity sliders. Return estimate (pure, planet-only potential `Φp = planet_potential_eff`, `rc = carrier_orbit_radius`): `climb_energy(r, speed, mass) = per_dv() · mass · (√(0.9·|Φp(rc) − Φp(r)|) + speed) · return_margin`, speed being |vel| in the rotating frame (the carrier rests there only at the default `carrier_patrol_speed` 0; the estimate does not account for a patrolling carrier). `HUD` uses `per_dv`-based `climb_energy` (full |vel|) for the tank reserve strip and frame, `Tow.fee` for the tow price; the week-4 carrier arrow reuses it. The estimate is a playtest hypothesis: planet-only, no climb simulation, and with full |v| it ignores that flying upward helps, so it overestimates (accepted; `return_margin` is the knob).
- Mass in `step`: `k = ref_mass / Hold.mass(s)` scales thrust (incl. boost), the soft brake accel and the drift-sync limit (reference branch and `_band_drift_accel`; the velocity-matching target is not scaled). Gravity and `frame_accel` are not scaled. A fresh `ShipState` has mass 10 = `ref_mass`, so k = 1.
- Thrust, ramp and heat (D36): holding the engine (thrust or boost) grows `hold_time`; the thrust scale is `lerp(ramp_min_fraction, 1, hold_time / ramp_time)`, so a short tap is a weak but non-zero impulse. Every other branch resets `hold_time`. `heat` in [0, 1] grows on the engine (faster on boost) and cools on every non-engine branch, so brakes never heat; above `heat_threshold` thrust falls linearly to `min_thrust_fraction` at full heat (boost inherits). `boost` alone gives `thrust_accel·boost_accel_mult`.
- Fuel drain (in `step`, per active branch, before the velocity update): `need = |a| · Hold.mass(s) · dt · Fuel.per_dv() · mult`, where `a` is the branch accel after the mass scaling, ramp and heat (boost included), `mult` is 1 for the engine, `boost_fuel_mult` for boost, `brake_fuel_mult` for soft brake and `sync_fuel_mult` for drift sync. `got = Hold.drain(s, need)`, and the accel is scaled by `f = got / need` (0 when nothing drained), so the run-out tick is partial and prediction stays exact there. Counters `fuel_engine` / `fuel_boost` / `fuel_soft_brake` / `fuel_sync` += `got` of their branch, in energy units (per-category breakdown; nothing outside tests reads them). The drain is by force, not accel: energy per Δv grows with mass, a clean climb costs ≈ `climb_coefficient` × mass, and a stable orbit (no thrust) costs nothing.
- No energy = engine off: when `Hold.energy(s) <= Hold.EPS` at the start of the tick, thrust and boost count as released (heat cools, `hold_time` resets, the ramp restarts once energy appears); soft brake and drift sync give zero accel. With no energy the ship drifts; an empty tank does not block the tow call (Tow).
- Atmosphere: static `ShipFlight.atmosphere_density(pos)` = `clamp((edge − |pos|)/(edge − surface), 0, 1) ^ density_shape`, radii from `Tuning.field`, shape from `Tuning.planet`, read per call; 0 at and above the edge, 1 at the surface.
- Atmospheric drag: in `step`, right before the velocity update, `vel *= exp(−atmosphere_drag·ρ·dt)` with ρ at the current position, for every control branch. The atmosphere co-rotates with the carrier frame, so rotating-frame `vel` is the velocity relative to the air. Unforced flight low in the atmosphere spirals down (default drag 0.5: about −3.7 u in 900 steps from r = 101250 on the D35 field). The default drag must give a measurable descent: float32 positions round radial motion slower than ~0.25 u/s per tick to zero (`docs/production/tech-debt.md` #11), so a weak drag stalls the spiral. Accepted cost: drift sync and its band-drift fallback fight the drag inside the atmosphere at fuel cost.
- Ring drag (M6): right after atmospheric drag, before the velocity update, inside the ring band the velocity relaxes toward the ring flow: `vel += (ring_flow(pos) − vel)·(1 − exp(−dt / ring_drag_tau))`, with the public statics `in_ring_band(pos)` (|r − `ring.radius`| ≤ `width/2`, false with an empty ring) and `ring_flow(pos)` = `WellGenerator.relative_omega(ring.radius)` × tangential. Every control branch, no fuel charged, no hull effect: the band catches the ship, it does not damage it (drag only by decision). Drift sync to a boulder zeroes v_rel, so hovering in the ring costs nothing beyond sync fuel. Ship only: free bodies (pieces, containers) are not dragged. The same code serves live flight and the prediction snapshot, so prediction == flight through the band.
- Hull and crash: after the position update `hull = max(hull − atmosphere_damage·ρ·dt, 0)` with ρ at the new position (no damage above the edge) and `crashed = |pos| <= planet_surface_radius or hull <= 0`. `step` only sets the flag and `Ship` ends the flight (no respawn), so the crash point stays visible to prediction and `step` owns no counter or node.
- Moon contact (`_moon_contact`): after the position update, for every body with `radius` > 0 (moons) at `GravityField.body_pos(i, t)`: if the ship is inside the disc it is put on the surface along the outward normal, and if it moves into the surface (velocity relative to `body_vel` has a negative normal part v_n) the normal part is reflected with restitution `moon_bounce` and `hull −= moon_impact_damage · |v_n|` (floored at 0). Both contacts share `_contact(s, n, v_n, bounce, damage)`: when `-v_n < Tuning.planet.rest_speed` (default 0.3) the ship is resting, only the normal part of the relative velocity is cancelled (no bounce, no damage), so lying on a surface does not drain hull; a harder impact bounces and damages as above. A moon never sets `crashed` by itself (only the planet surface or hull 0 does). Inside `step`, so prediction == flight and the prediction line bounces off moons; `Tuning.planet` is read per call. Accepted cost: push-out against a moving disc may jitter; a prediction == flight test guards determinism.
- Asteroid contact (`_asteroid_contact`): right after `_moon_contact`, for every asteroid within `max(ast_radius_min, ast_radius_max)` of the ship (`AsteroidField.near`, or the snapshot below) the asteroid is a circle of its `radius` at its position at t: inside it the ship is put on the surface along the outward normal, and a negative normal part v_n of the velocity relative to the asteroid `vel` is reflected with `asteroid_bounce`, `hull −= asteroid_impact_damage · |v_n|` (floored at 0). It never sets `crashed`. The 3D rock of `AsteroidView` is visual only; no physics-engine colliders (they would diverge prediction from flight). Resting on an asteroid is handled by the shared `_contact` helper like moons (`rest_speed`: normal cancelled, no bounce, no damage).
- Asteroid snapshot: `ShipFlight.step(state, input, t, dt, snapshot = null)`: without a snapshot (live flight) the asteroid queries are `AsteroidField.near(pos, t, R)` once per query; with one, its entries (`near` output carrying `r`, `theta`, `omega`) are moved to t by formula (`AsteroidField.at`) and filtered by the same radius, with identical results. `sync_reference(pos, t, snapshot = null)` takes it too.
- Beam (`ShipFlight._beam`, in `step` after the position update and before `_moon_contact`; D40): public `ShipFlight.beam_candidates(state, t, snapshot)` (also used by `BeamView`) are the unbroken asteroids within `beam_range + max(ast_radius_min, ast_radius_max)` plus non-container `s.bodies` with `radius` > `Tuning.planet.collect_radius` (bodies as `{key: id, pos, points, rot: 0, body}`); collectible bodies (radius <= `collect_radius`) are never hit. While `input.beam` the target is `Beam.hit(pos, input.aim, beam_range, candidates)`; `s.beam_target` / `s.beam_charge` grow by `beam_damage·dt` on the same target and reset to null / 0 on release, a miss or a different target. At `beam_charge >= break_per_sqrt_radius · sqrt(R_eq)`, `R_eq = sqrt(AsteroidField._area(points) / PI)`, the target breaks: an asteroid key goes to `s.broken`, a body is removed from `s.bodies`, and `Split.pieces(parent, split_count, piece_scatter, piece_spread, split_area_divisor)` are appended to `s.bodies` (an asteroid becomes a temporary `FreeBody` with its key as `id`). A piece with `radius` > `collect_radius` is breakable again, so breaking recurses down to collectible pieces (r120 → 56 → 27 → 13 at the defaults, up to 27 pieces). The threshold grows with sqrt of the area radius, not with area: an area threshold made an r20 hull break in ≈ 0.07 s. At the default k 0.5 an r20 hull takes about 1.55–2.11 s at `beam_damage` 1; accepted cost: big asteroids break slower, tunable by the slider. The charge belongs to the current target, not to the body, so a body cannot be finished in bursts. The beam adds nothing to the hold. Inside `step`, so prediction == flight (prediction narrowing uses `beam_range + q` while the beam is held).
- `ShipState.broken` (`Dictionary`, `Vector3i` key `(band, chunk, idx)` -> true) starts empty and is deep-copied by `copy()`; an asteroid in it is gone. `ShipState.beam_target` (null or key / body id) and `beam_charge` are copied too. `Beam` (`class_name`, static, pure): `layer(entry, broken)` returns the entry unchanged, or `{}` when its key is broken (no shrinking); `hit(pos, aim, range_, candidates) -> {key, point}` is the nearest intersection of the ray (aim is a world angle or direction vector) with the polygon edges (points rotated by `rot`, placed at `pos`), `{}` on a miss, beyond range, or through the gap between hull and `radius`.
- `Split.gd` (`class_name`, static, pure): `pieces(parent: FreeBody, n: int, scatter, spread, divisor) -> Array[FreeBody]` returns n pieces: `radius` = parent r / sqrt(divisor · n), so the pieces together cover the parent area / divisor (≈ 0.47 r at n = 3, divisor 1.5); the divisor applies to the total, not to each piece, since a parent / 1.5 piece shrinks r only 0.82× per break (about 9 breaks and thousands of pieces), hull from an RNG seeded by `hash([parent.id, i])` via `AsteroidField._hull`, centred at `parent.pos + dir · spread · R_eq` with `R_eq = sqrt(AsteroidField._area(parent.points) / PI)` (the parent's area radius) and `dir` on angle `i/n · TAU` plus a seeded phase, `vel` = parent `vel` + outward × `scatter`, `amount` = parent amount / n (the sum is exact; the resource is conserved while the area is not, so the hold economy does not depend on the divisor), `type` kept, `id` = `hash([parent.id, i, "piece"])`, `piece = true`, `age = 0`, `radius0` = the piece radius. The same parent gives identical pieces. The offset is from the parent's area radius, not the piece radius, so the split reads as coming from the centre.
- Collector (`ShipFlight._collect`, in `step` after `SmallObjects.update`; D40): while `input.collect` the sector has its apex at the ship, direction `input.aim`, radius 0.5 × `beam_range` and full angle `collect_angle`. A collectible body (radius <= `collect_radius`, or `container` = true once ready) in the sector whose type the hold can take (`Hold.room` > 0) gets `pull_speed += collect_accel·dt` and moves toward the ship by `pull_speed·dt` (never past it); only `pos` changes, its own `vel` stays. Any other body, or every body when the collector is released, gets `pull_speed` 0. The collector never breaks anything and reads only `s.bodies`, never asteroids, so `ShipPrediction` does not widen its query for it. It works over any `FreeBody`, containers included.
- Asteroid query contract (`ShipFlight._asteroids(pos, t, radius, snapshot, broken)`): both branches (live `AsteroidField.near`, or snapshot moved by an inline copy of the `AsteroidField.at` formula, which must change together with it) return the same entries `{key, pos, vel, radius, points, rot, type, amount}`, `key = Vector3i(band, chunk, idx)`, `rot = omega·t`, `broken` applied through `Beam.layer` (broken ones dropped), sorted by key (`_key_less`). Contact, drift-sync reference (`sync_reference(pos, t, snapshot, broken)`; `ReferenceMarker` passes the ship's `broken`) and the beam all read it, so a broken asteroid is neither hit, contacted, drawn nor a reference.
- Soft brake (`ship_brake`) lives in `step`, so prediction stays equal to flight. Its acceleration is half of `brake_accel` against velocity plus half backwards from the nose; near rest only the nose half is applied. Thrust and boost are ignored while it is held, rotation still works, no heat.
- Drift sync (`ship_drift_sync`): the reference is the pure `ShipFlight.sync_reference(pos, t, snapshot = null) -> {valid, index, is_carrier, asteroid}`; candidates are all bodies, the carrier point (`carrier_pos(t)`) and asteroids within `sync_radius` (`AsteroidField.near`), the nearest by distance to the centre strictly within `sync_radius` wins (a body on a tie); `asteroid` is `{pos, vel}` when an asteroid won and `{}` otherwise (`index` -1, `is_carrier` false), recomputed each step and never stored. With a reference the ship is pulled to the reference velocity at no more than `sync_accel`; the velocity comes from the public static `ShipFlight.reference_vel(ref, t)` — the asteroid's own `vel`, `carrier_vel(t)` for the carrier, `body_vel(index, t)` for a body, zero for an invalid reference — and `ReferenceMarker` reads the same static, so the marker's chevron and the sync pull can never disagree. Thrust and boost are ignored, no heat, rotation works, fuel is more expensive than the engine (`sync_fuel_mult`); with no energy the sync gives zero accel.
- Drift-sync fallback (no reference within `sync_radius`, `_band_drift_accel`): converge to the band drift of the current depth around `reference_body` (= `GravityField.dominant`). The math is done in the rotating frame: the orbit direction is taken from the inertial relative velocity (so a retrograde ship stays retrograde; near-radial velocity falls back to counter-clockwise) and the target is the circular velocity around the body expressed back in the rotating frame. It is clamped by `sync_accel`, independent of the nose, and billed like sync. Accepted costs: near a moon the planet pull also acts, so the target is not exactly circular; a reference switch jumps the target, smoothed only by the clamp.
- `ReferenceMarker` marks the sync reference: a `Palette.HALO` ring at the reference body, carrier point (`carrier_pos(t)`) or asteroid (`ref.asteroid.pos`), hidden when there is no reference. The ring has a constant screen size (world radius = `RING_PX * s`), brighter while `ship.input.drift_sync`. It re-reads the reference every physics tick (`update_reference(ref, t)`), which also stores the reference velocity (`ShipFlight.reference_vel(ref, t)`). View-only: reads the ship, never writes flight state.
- The reference carries the ship's speed chevron, showing the reference's OWN velocity (the speed the ship would be dragged to by the sync), in the ship's chevron colour: the back edge sits on the ring (`RING_PX` 28 px), the tip `CHEVRON_DEPTH_PX` 5 px further out (33 px), the |v| label `LABEL_OFFSET_PX` 24 px past the tip (57 px from the centre), all screen px times the semantic zoom. It is shown only on a close view (semantic zoom `s < CHEVRON_ZOOM_LIMIT` 15, a node constant, not a `Tuning.view` knob) and only for a reference moving at least 1 u/s (the ship's own `vector_visible` threshold), so a still planet or a stationary carrier carries no chevron. No other object carries a chevron: the moons, the carrier and the asteroid groups are unmarked even far away. The label is upright: it is compensated against the CAMERA's rotation (`FlightCamera.rotation_for(cam_pos)`, the camera follows the ship), not against the angle of the reference around the planet — otherwise the glyphs stand tilted. The marker preloads `FlightVector.gd` (it has no `class_name`) to reuse the shared chevron geometry, the label writer and the chevron colour. Accepted cost: the |v| label of a reference close to the ship can overlap the ship's own label (both are anchored in screen px from their centres) — a playtest item.
- `FlightCamera.gd` (`class_name`, Camera2D in `Run.tscn`) is the local-vertical camera (D35). Pure statics: `rotation_for(pos)` makes screen-down point at the planet center from any position; semantic zoom s ("s times more world per screen", 1x = 1280x800 units) is clamped to [1, 40], continuous up to 8 and then stops 10 / 15 / 22 / 32 / 40; `zoom_vector(s)` inverts it for `Camera2D.zoom`. The node follows the ship, rotates by `rotation_for` (`ignore_rotation = false` in `_ready`; Godot 4 defaults to true, which silently drops the rotation), eases s in s-space, and reads `camera_zoom_in` / `camera_zoom_out`. Autoscale picks the zoom by itself, from speed only: pure `auto_zoom(speed)` is linear from 1 at `autoscale_speed_low` (20 u/s) to `autoscale_max` (7.0) at `autoscale_speed_high` (170 u/s), clamped outside (25 u/s per level: 45 gives 2, 70 gives 3, 145 gives 6; stops 10..40 are manual only). There is no boundary term, no nearest-object term and no `boundary_distance`. The node feeds it (smoothed, below) `|vel − reference_vel|` with a valid `ShipFlight.sync_reference`, else `|vel − ShipFlight.band_drift_vel(pos, vel, t)|` (the `v_target` of `_band_drift_accel`, so raw speed deep in the well does not zoom out). The speed is smoothed before it is used: the node keeps `_speed_avg`, updated every `update` (also during a manual pause, so it is current when autoscale takes over) by the static `smooth_speed(avg, speed, delta) = lerp(avg, speed, 1 − exp(−delta / autoscale_speed_tau))` (`autoscale_speed_tau` 5.0 s, frame-rate independent), from the relative speed of `rel_speed()`, and the camera eases continuously toward `auto_zoom(_speed_avg)` — the delay lives in the speed, not in the zoom, so there are no steps while accelerating and a short dip in speed barely moves the zoom. When autoscale takes over after a pause the zoom starts from the current value (the ease handles it). `ease_zoom(current, target, k, delta)` is `1 − exp(−k·delta)` in s-space: `autoscale_smooth` for the autoscale target, the fast `ZOOM_SMOOTH` for a manual one. A zoom press (`on_zoom_press(dir)`) steps the target from the current zoom and pauses autoscale for `autoscale_override_time` of real time (`manual_left`); further presses restart the timer, and at 0 autoscale takes over from the current zoom. While the manual target is at or above `ZOOM_CONTINUOUS_MAX` (8) the pause does not run down (autoscale stays off); once a press brings it below 8 the normal pause counts from that press. The pause start adds 1 to `Telemetry.autoscale_overrides`, presses inside it do not. `update(delta)` (called from `_process`) holds the logic, so it is testable without `Input`. `_process` touches only zoom easing, position and rotation: the ship renders at honest world size (the whole look ≈ 32 u span, so its screen size is `VISUAL_SPAN · cam.zoom.x` = 32/s px, the 9..15 px fade band crossed at s ≈ 2.1..3.55); `ship.scale` and the collision shape are never touched. - `FreeBody.gd` (`class_name`, `RefCounted`): a small convex body, `pos`, `vel`, `radius`, `points` (local hull), `type` (asteroid type key), `amount`, `id`, `piece` (false; true for pieces of a broken body), `container` (false; true for a jettisoned hold cell), `age` (0, seconds since the break or the drop), `radius0` (0, the piece radius at birth; `SmallObjects.update` sets it to `radius` when 0), `pull_speed` (0, collector pull speed), `copy()` (deep, `points` duplicated, `radius0` and `container` copied). `FreeBody.integrate(b, t, dt)` is static and does what the ship does with no thrust: `vel += (GravityField.accel(pos, t) + GravityField.frame_accel(pos, vel)) · dt; pos += vel · dt`, so a body and an idle ship on equal start follow the same path. Pieces (via `Split`) and containers use it.
- `SmallObjects.gd` (`class_name`, static, pure, no scene dependency; part of `ShipFlight.step`): free bodies are flight state, not a formula of t, because free flight in the field has no closed form and M5 / M7 objects are created by actions. Nothing spawns on a schedule (D41: scheduled M4 bodies spawned too far away on elongated orbits to be dodged and added little); `s.bodies` holds beam-split pieces and jettisoned containers. `update(s, t, dt)` integrates every body with `FreeBody.integrate`, adds dt to `age` of `piece` bodies and drops a piece at `age >= L`, `L = piece_lifetime · max(1, radius0 / collect_radius)` (computed per check, not stored; r₀ ≤ 20 lives 100 s, r₀ 40 200 s, r₀ 60 300 s at the defaults; linear in radius, not area, so large pieces do not live 400–900 s). Over L a piece's area falls linearly from its birth area to the area of radius `piece_min_radius`: `k(t) = 1 − (1 − k_end) · t / L`, `k_end = min(1, (piece_min_radius / radius0)²)`; each step applies `f = k(age + dt) / k(age)` as `amount *= f`, `radius *= √f`, `points *= √f`. The shrink is incremental, not recomputed from birth values, so a partial pickup that lowered `amount` is never handed back; L and `k_end` use `radius0`, so they stay fixed while `radius` shrinks. A piece born at or below `piece_min_radius` keeps its size until L. The ore left at L (fraction `k_end`) is lost (accepted); the hull points are reallocated per piece per step, also in the prediction (accepted, within the 8-piece budget). A piece that shrinks to `collect_radius` or below becomes collectible and leaves the beam candidates by the existing rules. It then removes non-container bodies beyond `piece_despawn_radius` from the ship, and any body inside the atmosphere ceiling or inside a moon. Bodies pass through asteroids and each other.
- Small-body contact (in `ShipFlight.step`, after `_asteroid_contact`, before the atmosphere and crashed lines): contact when |ship − body| < `Tuning.ship.hit_radius` + body radius. A container follows its own rule (see Container lifetime and pickup). Any other collectible body (radius <= `collect_radius`) is always taken with no damage, collector held or not: `Hold.add` of its type and amount, any remainder stays in the body, and with a full hold it passes through harmlessly. Otherwise, radius < `piece_swallow_radius`: the body is removed, hull −= `ShipFlight.impact_damage(|v_ship − v_body|)`, ship velocity unchanged. Otherwise the ship is moved out to `hit_radius + radius` along the normal and bounces with `piece_bounce` relative to the body velocity through the resting / bounce logic of `_contact`, with hull −= `impact_damage(−v_n)` (push damage uses the normal approach speed, so a graze is nearly free) instead of the linear damage; swallow uses the full relative speed `|v_ship − v_body|`. Damage is relative to the body, not the band flow, so a slow ship still takes damage from a fast body. The body is not changed. `ShipFlight.impact_damage(v_rel) = impact_k · max(0, v_rel − v_safe)²` is the D40 quadratic rule; asteroid and moon contacts stay linear until M12. With empty `bodies` flight equals the flight without small objects. Prediction runs the same step over the copied `bodies`, so the line shows the deflection and a new piece or a pulled piece makes it jump (accepted). Accepted: pieces between `collect_radius` and `piece_swallow_radius` (r 20..25 at the defaults) are swallowed with damage; one large asteroid can leave up to 27 bodies at once, which raises step and prediction cost (tech-debt #54, #57), bounded by `piece_despawn_radius`, the piece lifetime and collection.

Surplus instances are hidden when bodies disappear. View-only.

- `Ship.gd` holds the authoritative `state`. `signal respawned` is emitted at the end of `respawn()`, after `Telemetry.begin_flight()`. `_ready` registers the node as `Telemetry.ship` (cleared in `_exit_tree`). A respawn while FLYING with an open flight of at least 1 s first writes a `reset` record via `Telemetry.end_flight` (no `flight_ended`). After each FLYING step `Telemetry.sample(state, input, report)` is called. `_ready` calls `respawn()`, also connected to `Tuning.preset_applied` (a preset load respawns at the carrier of the new field; generation-affecting slider edits go through `Tuning.regenerate` and respawn too, other slider edits do not). `respawn()` puts a fresh `ShipState` with `hull = Tuning.ship.hull_max` on the patrolling carrier point `carrier_pos(time())`, with the nose AT the planet and co-moving with the carrier while descending toward it: `planet_dir = (Vector2.ZERO - pos).normalized()`, `vel = carrier_vel(time()) + Tuning.ship.spawn_descend_speed · planet_dir`, `rot = planet_dir.angle() + PI / 2` (wrapped to [-PI, PI]), so `UP.rotated(rot)` points at the planet center — the velocity sums the carrier's patrol motion and the descend speed, so at the default `carrier_patrol_speed = 0` the carrier rests and it is the pure descend speed. `_physics_process` builds a `ShipInput` from the actions (an empty `ShipInput` while `state.docked`, so flame, prediction, beam and collector views go quiet), keeps it in `input`, steps with the fixed `GravityField.DT` and `GravityField.time()` (read after the tick increment, like `Body`), then, on the tick `state.docked` becomes true, writes `Telemetry.docking_time = state.approach_time` and with `--flight-log` prints `FLOG docked t=… docking_time=…`, then does `move_and_collide(state.pos - position)` and `rotation = state.rot`. Accepted cost: with no colliders `state.pos` is the node position; once something collides they diverge and need syncing.
- Flight phase: `Ship` has `enum Phase { FLYING, ENDED }`, `phase`, `flight_start_t` (both reset by `respawn()`: `FLYING`, `GravityField.time()`) and `signal flight_ended(summary: Dictionary)`. On the first tick the stepped state is `crashed` or `docked`, after the move it sets `phase = ENDED`, publishes an empty `ShipInput` and calls `Telemetry.end_flight(summary)` (writes the flight JSON, `autoload/CLAUDE.md`) and then emits `flight_ended` once with `FlightSummary.build(state, outcome, time() − flight_start_t, tow)`: outcome `"crashed"` (also `Telemetry.crashes += 1`; a crash wins a same-tick dock, as in `step`) or `"returned"`; a tow arrival ends the flight the same way with `"towed"` (Tow). A flight ends with an outcome, never with an instant respawn. In `ENDED` the ship is not stepped and `input` stays empty (thrust does not undock): a crashed ship stays at the crash point, a docked one keeps tracking `carrier_pos(t)` / `carrier_vel(t)`. Only the next flight (`Tuning.regenerate` → `preset_applied` → `respawn()`, at the carrier with fresh cargo and tank) leaves `ENDED`. `flight_ended` fires only on FLYING → ENDED: a preset load or regenerate mid-flight respawns without it, so preset loads make no fake flights. The phase lives in `Ship`, not in `ShipState`: the summary is not flight physics and prediction would copy it. Accepted: the world keeps running behind an ended ship; a moon or asteroid crossing it has no effect, since it is not stepped.
- `FlightSummary.gd` (`class_name`, `RefCounted`, pure static): `build(state, outcome, flight_time, tow := {}) -> Dictionary` gives `{outcome, cargo: {type: mass}, cargo_lost: bool, fuel: {engine, boost, soft_brake, sync}, time, seized: {type: mass}, seized_share, tow_kind}`; `tow_kind` is the tow dict's `kind` (`"manual"` / `"ceiling"` / `"forced"`) for `"towed"`, else `""`; `seized` and `seized_share` come from the `Tow.pay` result (`seized`, `share`) and are `{}` / 0.0 when not towed. Cargo mass per type = Σ `fill · Tuning.resources[type].mass` over `state.hold` (empty hold, empty `cargo`); `cargo_lost` is `outcome == "crashed"` (a crash loses all cargo, which is still listed); fuel comes from `fuel_engine` / `fuel_boost` / `fuel_soft_brake` / `fuel_sync`. `outcome` is `"returned"` | `"crashed"` | `"towed"`. For a tow `build` runs after `Tow.pay`, so `cargo` is what remains.
- `ShipPrediction.run(state, input, tick, steps) -> PackedVector2Array` (`class_name`, static, pure) repeats `ShipFlight.step` with `t = GravityField.time_at(tick + i + 1)`, returning the position after each step; it stops at the first `crashed` or `docked` state, so the crash or dock point is the last point (fewer than N points), otherwise it returns N. It starts from `ship.state` and is bit-identical to real flight with the same input. It takes any N.
- Prediction snapshot: regenerating asteroid chunks on every step cost ~300 ms per 900-step run, so `run` takes an `AsteroidField.near` snapshot around the current position with radius `cover = (|v| + max_speed()) · remaining_steps·DT + q + 2·NARROW_MARGIN`, where `max_speed()` is `AsteroidField.max_speed()` (asteroids move toward the ship too) and `q` is `max(ast_radius_min, ast_radius_max)` (or at least `sync_radius` when `drift_sync` is held). The snapshot is re-taken when `|pos − c0| + |v|·DT + 1 + max_speed()·(t − t0) + q + NARROW_MARGIN` exceeds `cover` (thrust and gravity change `v`, so one start-time bound is not enough), so every asteroid the ship can meet is in it and results equal live flight. The ship only meets asteroids near it, so each step runs on a narrowed copy (entries within `q + NARROW_MARGIN` of a centre, `NARROW_MARGIN` 800) that is rebuilt when `|pos − centre| + |v|·DT + 1 + max_speed()·Δt + q` could exceed `q + NARROW_MARGIN`; each step receives this narrowed copy as the snapshot; the copy is a pure filter, so results are unchanged. A cost note is in `docs/production/tech-debt.md` #6.
- `Prediction` is a `Palette.HALO` `Line2D` after `Ship` in `Run.tscn` (reads the already-stepped state), not a child of `Ship`. It is shown only while an engine action is held (`ship.input.thrust` / `boost` / `soft_brake` / `drift_sync`; W / Space / S / Shift): with none held it is hidden, no new task starts, and a completed result is reaped through the single wait path and discarded; on the false → true edge `_gen` is bumped and `points` cleared (no stale line from a previous hold), a fresh task starts as soon as the worker is free, ignoring `predict_every`. The line is computed from the static `Prediction.held_input(ship.input)`, a copy with `turn`, `jettison`, `jettison_select` and `transfer` zeroed (a held rotation would spin the replayed ship and degenerate the line to coasting; future drops are not shown); the heading target and state are kept. The width is constant screen size (`2 px / cam.zoom.x`, refreshed every frame). The main thread never runs `ShipPrediction.run`: `_physics_process` snapshots `ship.state.copy()`, `held_input(ship.input)`, the start tick T and `predict_short_steps` and starts one `WorkerThreadPool` task at a time (a dedicated `Thread` would need more code and a careful shutdown). A new task starts only when none runs and at least `maxi(predict_every, 1)` ticks passed since the last start, so `predict_every` is the minimum interval and the line lags one or two recomputes (accepted; only the main thread must stay free). The result is picked up on the single path `is_task_completed` → `wait_for_task_completion` (exactly once per task id, else the task leaks) and trimmed to start at the ship: at tick N, `points = result.slice(N − T)` (empty when `N − T >= size`), so the line at tick N is not `run(state_N)`; `ShipPrediction.run` itself stays bit-identical to flight. The task writes its result into member fields with no mutex; the completion check orders the access. On the ship's `respawned` signal (a preset load or regeneration, the next flight included), not on `preset_applied`, a generation counter `_gen` is incremented and `points` cleared: a result of an older generation is dropped, so no stale line is ever shown, the line is empty until the first fresh run, and a fresh task starts as soon as the worker is free, ignoring `predict_every` (no synchronous run, which would hitch ≈ 22 ms per respawn). The task holds `Tuning.lock` for the whole run (`autoload/CLAUDE.md`). `_exit_tree` waits for a running task, so a freed node never receives a worker write. A scalar `Tuning` write during a run gives the worker a mix of old and new values (accepted: the next run corrects the line). There is no full-trajectory mode; the Otryv scene will call `ShipPrediction.run` with its own N. The line ends at a crash point (planet surface or hull 0) or at the docking capture point. The line bounces off moons like flight. Recompute cost is `docs/production/tech-debt.md` #6.

- Docking (in `ShipFlight.step`, pure state, so prediction == flight up to the dock; knobs in `Tuning.planet`, `autoload/CLAUDE.md`): the pure static `ShipFlight.carrier_pull(offset, v_rel) -> Vector2` (offset = pos − `carrier_pos(t)`, v_rel = vel − `carrier_vel(t)`, d = |offset|) is 0 for d >= `capture_radius`; inside it is w · (−ω²·offset − 2ω·v_rel) with w = 1 − d / `capture_radius` (0 at the edge, 1 at the centre) and ω = `pull_omega`, a critically damped spring because a pure radial pull oscillates and orbits; |a| is clamped to `pull_accel_max`, below the thrust, so the engine overpowers it. The pull uses no fuel and ignores mass, like gravity, acts in every control branch and is added to the acceleration before the velocity update, only while `dock_armed`; input works the whole time inside the zone. A fresh `ShipState` is not `dock_armed` (the spawn sits at the carrier); it arms, for good, on the first tick at or beyond `capture_radius` from the carrier, so the pull is off until the first exit. After the crash line an armed ship with d < `dock_radius` and |v_rel| < `dock_speed` docks: pos / vel snap to exactly `carrier_pos(t)` / `carrier_vel(t)` and `docked` is set; `docked` keeps tracking the carrier with zero relative velocity; there is no undock, the next flight respawns the ship. Not an `Area2D`: prediction could not see it. `approach_time` grows by dt every tick while |pos − carrier| < `approach_radius` (the docked tick included) and resets to 0 the tick the ship is outside; it is the docking time metric (approach-radius entry to docked). If the ship never left the radius after the spawn, the value counts from the spawn (accepted). Accepted: a docked ship skips contacts while moons and asteroids pass by; a fast pass through the zone may slingshot instead of docking; float32 noise near R_c (tech-debt #11) may jitter the last metres, `dock_radius` / `dock_speed` leave margin.
- `LongPress.gd` (`class_name`, `RefCounted`, pure; M7, D42): params `tap_time`, `hold_time`, `interval`; `update(pressed, dt)` returns `NONE`, `TAP` (released before `tap_time`), `FIRE` (at `hold_time`, then every `interval` while held) or `CANCEL` (released in [`tap_time`, min(`tap_time` + 1 s, `hold_time`))); other releases give `NONE`. `Ship` owns one built from `Tuning.ship.jettison_*` (`jettison_tap_time` 0.7, `jettison_hold_time` 2.0, `jettison_interval` 0.7, `jettison_push` 5, `jettison_pickup_delay` 1.5), feeds it from action `ship_jettison` (X, joypad button 1) with the real step `TimeWarp.real_step(warp_k)` (`GravityField.DT / warp_k`, so holds and the repeat interval are real seconds under warp), turns TAP / FIRE into one-tick `ShipInput.jettison_select` / `jettison` and counts `Telemetry.jettison_cancelled` on every CANCEL and `jettison_selects` / `jettisons` only when the hold is non-empty at TAP / FIRE. A release before `tap_time` is a select, not a cancel. The press timers live on the node, not in `ShipState`, so state copies stay cheap and the prediction never replays a held drop.
- Jettison in `step` (`ShipFlight._jettison`, after the position update and before `_beam`): `jettison_select` sets `ShipState.jettison_pick` to `Hold`'s next pick (-1 = heaviest cell, fill x type mass, ties to the lowest index; empty hold: no change); `jettison` removes the effective `s.hold` cell (never `s.tank`), resets the pick to -1 and appends a container `FreeBody` (`container = true`, `piece = false`, square `points`, radius 0.5 x `collect_radius`, the cell's type and fill, ship pos, velocity ship vel - nose x `jettison_push`). Inside `step`, so prediction == flight.
- Transfer (M9): `ShipFlight._transfer(s, input)` runs in `step` next to `_jettison`: with `input.transfer` it calls `Hold.transfer(s)`, one ice cell per tick in any flight state; `ShipInput.transfer` is a one-tick field (in `copy()`), zeroed by `Prediction.held_input`. Input action `ship_transfer` = R / joypad X (button 2). `Ship` owns a second `LongPress` (`transfer_hold_time` 1.0 s, `transfer_interval` 0.7 s from `ShipParams`, recreated in `respawn()`); FIRE sets `input.transfer`; after the step `Ship` increments `Telemetry.transfers` when the step reported `transferred` (the actual `Hold.transfer` outcome, so a same-tick jettison taking the last ice cell is not counted and a slot freed by the same tick's drain is); TAP / CANCEL are ignored. Thrust works from a tank holding only raw ice (energy gate above).
- Container lifetime and pickup: `SmallObjects` ages containers by `dt` without shrink or expiry, and skips them in the `piece_despawn_radius` removal; atmosphere and moons still remove them. `_collect` pulls and `_small_contact` takes a container into the hold only while `input.collect` and `age >= jettison_pickup_delay` (`_pickup_ready`, 1.5 s); remainder stays on partial pickup; otherwise no contact, push or damage. Containers are always collectible (no radius test) and never beam candidates (`beam_candidates` excludes them by the `container` flag, not radius), whatever `collect_radius` is set to; they are not drift-sync references. Pickup needs the collector and the delay because touch pickup would re-absorb a fresh drop and bypass the collector (D40); dropping is never blocked while collecting, so the emergency press cannot silently fail. Accepted: with a full hold a container is not collected and stays in the field. Containers draw through `SmallObjectView` as 3D boxes over their square in the type colour. `Ship.respawn()` resets the `LongPress`, so a press held through a crash or preset reload does not fire in the new flight.
- Tow: `Tow.gd` (`class_name`, `RefCounted`, pure static; N3, D43): `refusal(pos) -> String` is `""` when `|pos| >= WellGenerator.tiers().tow` and "ниже границы буксира" below it; the tow boundary always lies above the atmosphere edge, so there is no separate atmosphere check. `fee(s)` = `tow_base + tow_markup · energy_price · Fuel.climb_energy(|pos|, |vel|, Hold.mass(s))`: the whole climb from the ship's point, the same estimate as the HUD reserve, so it grows with depth and hold mass. `cargo_value(s)` = Σ hold `fill · price` (tank excluded). `seized_share(s)` = `min(1, fee / cargo_value)`, 0 for an empty hold. `pay(s)` takes the share first, multiplies every hold cell's fill by (1 − share) (no rounding, no whole-cell picks), drops cells left at ≤ `Hold.EPS`, clears the tank and returns `{seized: {type: mass}, share, fee, drained_energy}`. `pay(s, forced := true)` (N4) seizes everything: share 1.0, `fee` = `cargo_value(s)`, tank drained. `violation(pos, boat) -> String` (N4): `"ceiling"` when `|pos| > tiers().ceiling`, `"boundary"` when `boat` and `|pos| < tiers().tow`, else `""`.
- The tow is a rescue for a fuel miscalculation, never a cheaper way home with cargo (D43): the fee pays for the whole climb from the arrival point (not the energy deficit, since fuel can be spent closing in on the carrier) and the tank is drained completely, so a tow never beats climbing yourself; a small miss near the carrier is cheap, a full hold at the boundary loses almost all of it. The fee is taken at arrival, so flying toward the carrier while waiting lowers it. The fee never exceeds the cargo; the rest is written off, there is no debt. Accepted: `tow_base` / `tow_markup` are hypotheses and may make tows near-total or near-free (the HUD price shows it); jettisoning before arrival lowers the fee but loses that cargo anyway.
- Tow state lives in `Ship`, not `ShipState` (the wait does not change flight physics; prediction would copy it): `tow_left` (−1 = no call), `tow_notice` with `tow_notice_left` (the last refusal or break, shown `TOW_NOTICE_TIME` 2 s), `tow_holding` (`ship_tow` held) and `tow_share_preview` (`Tow.seized_share(state)` each tick while held or waiting), all reset by `respawn()`.
- Call: a third `LongPress` (`tap_time = jettison_tap_time`, `hold_time = tow_hold_time`, recreated in `respawn()`) reads `ship_tow` only while FLYING. FIRE with no call in progress is refused (notice) or accepted (`tow_left = tow_delay`, `Telemetry.tow_calls += 1`); other FIREs are ignored; TAP does nothing; CANCEL counts `Telemetry.tow_cancelled`. An accepted call is final (no cancel); the ship keeps flying during the wait.
- After each step: a crash or dock ends the flight as `"crashed"` / `"returned"` and drops the call, so a dock or crash on the arrival tick wins (accepted). Otherwise an active call below the boundary breaks with the refusal as notice (a call made exactly at the boundary may break on the next tick; accepted); else `tow_left -= DT`, and at ≤ 0 `Tow.pay` runs on the arrival state, `Telemetry.tow_fee` / `tow_seized_share` are written and the flight ends `"towed"` with the paid result passed to `FlightSummary.build`. One `flight_ended` per flight. Every tow dict carries `kind`: `"manual"` (this path), `"ceiling"`, `"forced"`.
- Violation timer (N4, D24, D35): `Ship.violation` (`""` / `"ceiling"` / `"boundary"`) and `violation_ticks` (int, reset by `respawn()`) count in physics ticks, not floats. Each FLYING tick after the crash / dock check, a change of `Tow.violation(state.pos, Tuning.ship.service_boat)` stores it and resets the ticks to 0 (coming back resets the countdown); while non-empty `violation_ticks += 1` and `tow_share_preview` is 1.0 for the boundary, else `Tow.seized_share(state)`. At `violation_ticks >= ceili(violation_time / DT)` the tow arrives at once, with no extra `tow_delay`: the flight ends `"towed"` (the outcome set stays three). Ceiling: `Tow.pay(state)` as the manual tow (kind `ceiling`, normal fee, `Telemetry.ceiling_tows += 1`); boat below the boundary: `Tow.pay(state, true)` (kind `forced`, all cargo seized, `Telemetry.forced_tows += 1`); `tow_fee` / `tow_seized_share` are written as for a manual tow. A ship that is not a service boat gets no timer below the boundary. `violation_left() -> float` is the remaining ticks × DT, −1 with no violation (read by the HUD, later by the warning bus). A pending manual call keeps running above the ceiling and whichever arrives first ends the flight (kind `manual`); below the boundary the manual refusal still cancels it. Accepted: the ceiling fee uses `Fuel.climb_energy` with `absf(dphi)`, so it is small but positive above the carrier.
- Time warp: with `Tuning.ship.time_warp` on, holding `ship_boost` while flying runs game time `time_warp_mult` times faster in real time; releasing it returns to normal speed at once. `Ship._read_input` then sets `inp.boost = false`, so boost never fires in warp mode (boost code, `fuel_boost` and its sound and summary rows are unchanged and used with `time_warp` off). The warp adds physics ticks, it never changes the step: pure static `TimeWarp.ticks_per_second(warp_mode, held, flying, mult) -> int` (`scenes/TimeWarp.gd`) is `BASE_TICKS` 60 × `max(mult, 1)` only with warp mode, held and flying, else 60; `TimeWarp.max_steps(ticks)` = `max(BASE_STEPS 8, ticks / 60 × 2)`. `Ship._apply_warp(flying)` writes both into `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second` / `Engine.max_physics_steps_per_frame`; it runs each FLYING tick before the step with `flying = not state.docked`, and with `flying` false (60 / 8) in `respawn()`, in the ENDED branch and in `_exit_tree`. `GravityField.DT` stays 1/60, so every tick is the same as without warp and prediction == flight holds; `Engine.time_scale` is not used because flight code ignores delta, and a bigger DT would break prediction == flight and the integrator's stability. `TimeWarp.factor(ticks)` = ticks / 60 (float) is the warp factor k and `TimeWarp.real_step(k)` = `DT / k` the real seconds per tick; `_apply_warp` stores k in `Ship.warp_k` (1 when not warping), which feeds the three `LongPress` updates (jettison, transfer, tow: real time, physics and prediction unchanged), `Telemetry.sample` (`real_time`, `warp_time`) and `Ship._approach_real` (real counterpart of `approach_time`: grows by `real_step` while `approach_time > 0`, else 0; written to `Telemetry.docking_real_time` on the docked tick). Accepted costs: everything else counted in ticks (tow countdown, violation timer, sound beats, heat) runs `mult` times faster in real time during warp, being game time; a slow frame at high `mult` may not fit its ticks and the game lags (the multiplier is a slider). Real-time views (camera ease, halo phase, warning display time) are not rescaled. Tests that drive boost through `Ship` input set `time_warp = false`.

## Sound (N6)
- Sound is procedural: no audio files, no downloads. `SoundBank` (`class_name`, static) builds `AudioStreamWAV` buffers once, 16-bit mono at 22050 Hz, deterministic (seeded noise), cached per name by `SoundBank.stream(name)`. Loops (`LOOP_FORWARD`): `engine` (low saw plus sub sine), `rough` (filtered noise), `boost` (bright noise plus saw), `hiss` (high noise), `tone` (sine ~660 Hz). One-shots (`LOOP_DISABLED`): `click`, `double` (two short beeps), `danger` (~880 Hz), `info` (~440 Hz), `thud` (low decaying noise), `chord` (major triad). Timbres are placeholders; samples can replace the streams without touching `SoundModel`.
- `SoundModel` (`class_name`, static, pure, tested) maps state to sound parameters: `thrust_level(s, inp)` in 0..1 (0 without thrust or with an empty tank, else `ShipFlight`'s ramp over `hold_time` times the heat falloff above `heat_threshold`; it repeats that formula, a test compares it with the acceleration `ShipFlight.step` gives; mass and boost are excluded); `engine_pitch(mass, level) = clamp(sqrt(ref_mass / max(mass, ε)) × lerp(0.8, 1.2, level), 0.4, 2.0)` (heavier is lower, more thrust is higher); `engine_volume(level)` in 0..1 with an idle floor of 0.15 while flying; `rough_mix(heat)` 0 below 0.5 rising linearly to 1 at heat 1; `boost_on(inp, level)`; `hiss_volume(density)` = density clamped to 0..1; `heat_click(prev, heat)` true on an upward crossing of `heat_threshold`; `crack_due(ticks)` true every `ceil(crack_period / DT)` ticks while atmosphere damage is positive; `reserve_mode(state: HUD.State)` is `&"click"` (CALM), `&"double"` (NEAR) or `&"tone"` (BELOW); `reserve_beat(mode, ticks)` fires on tick 0 and then every `reserve_click_period` (4 s) / `reserve_beep_period` (2 s), never for `tone` (a loop); `cue_for(type)` maps a warning type to a one-shot: `danger` for boundary, atmosphere, reserve, ceiling (and unknown types), `info` for tow, tow_refused, `thud` for impact.
- `SoundView` (`scenes/SoundView.gd`, Node) owns the players: one `AudioStreamPlayer` per loop, started once in `_ready` and switched off by volume 0 (`linear_to_db` with a -80 dB floor), so a loop never restarts; one player per one-shot. `_physics_process` reads `../Ship` (null-safe) and drives loop volumes and pitch from `SoundModel`: engine hum (pitch by mass and thrust, volume by thrust), rough layer by heat, boost roar, atmosphere hiss by `ShipFlight.atmosphere_density`, the `tone` for reserve BELOW. It fires the heat click, the atmosphere crack (`click` stream), reserve beats (the tick counter resets when the mode changes by `HUD.ship_reserve`) and the `chord` on the docked edge. Everything is silent while `phase == ENDED` and while docked; counters reset on `ship.respawned`.
- Every `Warnings.warning_shown(type)` (`../CanvasLayer/Warnings`, null-safe) plays exactly one cue, `play(cue_for(type))`. `play(name)` increments `played[name]` (Dictionary, for tests) and prints `FLOG sound …` when `Telemetry.flight_log`; reserve-mode changes print a `FLOG sound` line too.
- Each frame `SoundView` applies `Tuning.view.volume` to the Master bus (`set_bus_volume_db(0, linear_to_db(v))`, `set_bus_mute(0, v <= 0)`); the bus volume is global. Headless runs (Dummy audio driver) do not fail. Sound is not an autoload: it belongs to the flight scene.
- Non-goals: music, hub sounds, stereo cues.
