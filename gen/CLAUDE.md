# gen

Pure static generators for the well; no node state, no cache except the `tiers()` memo.

- `WellGenerator.tiers() -> Dictionary` is stage 1. It root-finds on `GravityField.planet_potential_eff` (3 bisections, ≈ 84 µs) and memoizes the result in static vars, keyed by the values it reads: `Tuning.field` `k`, `eps`, `fade_width`, `atmosphere_edge`, `carrier_orbit_radius`, `bodies[0]` `mass` / `p` / `influence_radius`, `Tuning.planet` `ceiling_offset` and a copy of `tier_shares`, all compared exactly on every call. Sliders write `Tuning` fields in place and `Tuning.apply` swaps resources without one change signal, so a value key (not a reset on a signal) keeps any change effective on the next call; a new input of `tiers()` must join the key. The memo makes per-step callers (`AsteroidField`, `WellView`) cheap. `tiers()` runs on the main thread and the `Prediction` worker at once, so a static `Mutex` `_lock` guards the freshness check plus the return of the memo and, separately, the store of the new memo; the computation runs outside the lock (two threads computing at once is harmless), and the lock is taken after `Tuning.lock`, never before. The returned dict and its `boundaries` are read-only (`make_read_only`), shared by all callers; a caller that mutates them errors (accepted). Result: `boundaries: Array[float]` (5 ascending radii: `atmosphere_edge`, then 10 %, 35 %, 75 % of the Φ_eff swing above the edge, then `carrier_orbit_radius`; cumulative `tier_shares` from the bottom), `tow` (= `boundaries[2]`, bottom of the middle tier) and `ceiling` (= `carrier_orbit_radius + ceiling_offset`).
- swing = Φp(`carrier_orbit_radius`) − Φp(`atmosphere_edge`); each inner boundary is found by bisection on [edge, r_c] and stops on a Φ tolerance relative to the swing, not an r tolerance: `frame_omega` uses `eps` while Φ does not, so g_eff(r_c) is only ≈ 0 and the profile is flat near r_c.
- `WellGenerator.tier_of(r) -> int` is the tier index (0 bottom .. 3 top) from `tiers()["boundaries"]`, clamped: 0 below the atmosphere edge, 3 above the carrier orbit.
- `WellGenerator.picture(seed, flight) -> Dictionary` (`{seed, flight, moons: Array[BodyParams], ring: {radius, width, phase}}`; `ring = {}` when no gap fits) builds the moons and the ring. `picture` computes `tiers()` once and passes the boundaries to `_build(seed, flight, bounds = [])` (static, test-visible; empty `bounds` means it computes them itself) for every retry and the fallback. `_build` seeds a `RandomNumberGenerator` with `hash([seed, flight])`, so a picture is deterministic and the same call gives the same result. Moon count is `randi_range(moon_count_min, moon_count_max)` (at least 1): one large moon (`radius` in `moon_large_radius_min/max`, orbit between `boundaries[3]` and `boundaries[4]`) and the rest small (`moon_small_radius_min/max`, orbit between `boundaries[1]` and `boundaries[3]`). Each moon has `parent` 0, p = 2, random `phase`, `mass = moon_mass_k · radius^moon_mass_exp`, `influence_radius = moon_influence_k · radius` and `angular_speed` of a circular orbit on the planet pull at its orbit radius (`accel_of(0, ...)`).
- Ring: after the moons `_ring` takes the tier `boundaries[ring_tier]..boundaries[ring_tier+1]` (`ring_tier` indexes `boundaries` bottom-up, unlike the top-down `tier_shares`), cuts out every moon annulus `orbit ± (influence_radius + moon_margin)`, keeps the gaps at least `ring_width` wide, picks one, a random radial offset inside it and a random `phase`. The ring is its own band: one Ω at its centre radius.
- `WellGenerator.relative_omega(r)` = `sqrt(g_planet(r)/r) − GravityField.frame_omega()` (planet-only g, as in `_moon`), read per call; ~0 at the carrier orbit. Rings, bands and asteroids use it.
- `WellGenerator.check(moons, ring = null) -> bool` accepts a layout when every moon clears the atmosphere (`orbit_radius − radius > atmosphere_edge`), the carrier orbit (`|r_c − orbit_radius| > influence_radius + moon_margin`) and every other moon (orbit gap > sum of influence radii + `moon_margin`). With a `ring` argument it also rejects an empty ring and a ring band crossing a moon annulus (`|ring.radius − orbit_radius| < influence_radius + moon_margin + width/2`); `check(moons)` alone keeps the moon-only result. `picture` tries `seed`, `seed + 1`, ... up to `max_tries` and returns the first layout passing `check(moons, ring)`; if none passes it builds `fallback_seed` (it is not checked again; a test pins that `fallback_seed` 2 passes on the default field and p1..p4 with the ring; seed 1 fails the ring-aware check). The returned `seed` is the seed actually used, so it may differ from the requested one.
- `AsteroidField` (`gen/AsteroidField.gd`, static, `RefCounted`) is the lazy asteroid generator; it reads `Tuning.planet`, `Tuning.field` and `Tuning.picture` on every call, no cache. Bands of `band_width` run from `atmosphere_edge` to the ceiling: `band_count()`, `band_of(r)` (clamped to `band_count() − 1`), `band_centre(k)` and `band_omega(k) = WellGenerator.relative_omega(band_centre(k))` (the last band may extend past the ceiling), except a band overlapping the ring annulus (`_ring_band(k)`): the ring is its own band, so such a band takes the ring's single Ω `relative_omega(ring.radius)`. Every object in a band rotates rigidly with the band Ω: one band per tier would shear visibly (relative Ω varies across a tier), and an Ω per object would break chunk content. `band_blocked(k)` is true when the band intersects a moon annulus (`orbit ± (influence_radius + moon_margin)`) or the ring band; such bands have no fields (time-independent, since bands and moons rotate at different Ω), and a ring band holds boulders instead (below). The band is widened by `field_radius + ast_radius_max` on each side, so a field placed near a band edge cannot reach a moon zone or the ring.
- Chunk = fixed arc in its band: `chunk_count(k) = round(2π·r_k/chunk_arc)` (at least 1), `Δθ = 2π/n`. `chunk(k, i, bands = -1, bounds = [])` is a pure function of `Tuning.picture` `seed` / `flight`, `k`, `i` (RNG `hash([seed, flight, k, i])`; `bands` only skips a recount, `bounds` are the tier boundaries, computed once by `near()` and passed in, empty means `chunk` fetches them from `WellGenerator.tiers()`): a ring band returns its boulders and no field (below); otherwise 0 or 1 field of 3..6 attempted asteroids (`FIELD_MIN/MAX`) inside `field_radius`, dart-throwing Poisson-disk placement (`TRIES` per asteroid), each asteroid a convex hull of random points (distance 0.85..1.0 of the radius from the centre, so the hull stays within `radius` and the contact circle) with radius in `ast_radius_min..ast_radius_max`. Entries are `{r, theta, radius, points, type, amount}` at t = 0. `type` is a `Tuning.resources` key by tier (an inline clamped tier walk over `bounds`, same result as `WellGenerator.tier_of(r)`: 0 `ore_unique`, 1 `ore_rare`, 2 `ore_middle`, 3 `ice` with probability `top_ice_share` else `ore_base`), decided by a hash draw `(hash([seed, flight, k, i, j, "comp"]) & 0xFFFFFF) / 2^24 < top_ice_share`, not by the chunk RNG, so hull geometry does not shift; `amount` = hull area (shoelace) × `amount_per_area` (cells). The chance of a field is `band_expected(k)` over the expected asteroids per chunk: `asteroids_per_band` for bands at least `drop_start` bands (`drop_start` is measured in bands from the carrier orbit) below the carrier, then each band closer to the carrier ×`drop_ratio`, bands above the carrier ×`above_factor` (so above the carrier the density is `asteroids_per_band · drop_ratio^drop_start · above_factor`). A field is placed at a random point in its chunk and may reach into neighbours.
- Ring boulders (M6): every band overlapping the ring annulus (`_ring_band`) generates boulders instead of a field, in the same entry shape and `Vector3i(band, chunk, idx)` space as asteroids. `_boulders(k, i)` shares the chunk RNG key `hash([seed, flight, k, i])` in its own branch, so no draws are shared with fields; `ring_boulders_per_chunk` is the expected boulders per chunk — the integer part always spawns, the fraction is one RNG draw, 0 leaves the ring band empty. A boulder: radius in `ast_radius_min..ast_radius_max`, convex hull, `r` inside the annulus ∩ band, angle inside its own chunk, `type` `ore_middle`, `amount` = hull area × `amount_per_area` — the ring band's mining mechanic, the middle-ore shelf of «Обод»: broken by the beam into collectible pieces and a drift-sync reference exactly like asteroids. Placement only is stored: pos / vel are formulas of t at `band_omega(k)` (the ring Ω), like asteroids. Key uniqueness relies on `band_blocked` keeping asteroid fields out of every band touching the ring annulus — if ring fields were ever allowed, `ShipState.broken` keys would collide (accepted coupling, documented in `chunk` / `_boulders`). `near`, `state` / `at` and `max_speed()` treat boulders like any band object.
- Motion is a formula of t: `state(k, ast, t)` / `at(ast, t)` give `pos = r·(cos, sin)(theta + Ω·t)` and `vel = Ω × pos`; `chunk_index(k, angle, t) = floor((angle − Ω·t)/Δθ) mod n`, so chunk membership never changes with t. `max_speed()` is the largest band speed.
- `near(pos, t, R) -> Array` returns exactly the asteroids within R of `pos` as `{pos, vel, radius, points, type, amount, band, chunk, idx, r, theta, omega}` (`r`, `theta` at t = 0 and the band `omega` let a caller move the entry to any t without regeneration). It visits only the bands and chunks the circle can reach (neighbour chunks and bands included). Cost: generating chunks is the expensive part (tech-debt #6), so callers with many steps take one `near` snapshot. `AsteroidField.max_speed()` is the largest band speed `|band_omega|·(band_centre + band_width/2 + field_radius)`; callers use it to bound how far an asteroid moves in a horizon.
- `Split.pieces` (M5, `scenes/CLAUDE.md`) reuses the private `AsteroidField._hull` for the hulls of pieces and `AsteroidField._area` for the parent's area radius, so a change of their signatures must change `Split` too.
